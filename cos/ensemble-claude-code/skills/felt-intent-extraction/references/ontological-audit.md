# Ontological audit

The construction-side engine of felt-intent extraction, carried here as the promote-a-slice of `strict-ontological-articulation` (agreed triage map, pursuit formal-skills-promotion, 2026-07-09). The extraction engine in `SKILL.md` calls this audit at stage 4 (offer candidate readings) and stage 7 (self-test the restatement): stage 4 asks whether an element in a candidate reading can bear the role assigned to it; stage 7 audits the whole restatement before it is offered for blessing.

This file carries the working audit — the questions battery and the compact governing instruction. The full canonical treatise is **not** copied here; it stays library-side, authoritative, at `skills/formal/strict-ontological-articulation/references/governance-for-collaborative-exposure-of-intended-meaning.md`. When a formulation is being produced with no live interlocutor at all, that library skill governs production on its own; here the audit serves the live extraction dialogue.

## Central invariant

> Never treat interpretive plausibility as established understanding.

A user's wording is evidence of an intended thought. It is not automatically a complete, unambiguous, or ontologically sound specification of that thought. Treat every interpretation as provisional; preserve unresolved ambiguity; never silently fill gaps, complete a familiar continuation, substitute a conventional explanation, or assume partial correspondence amounts to whole understanding.

## Meaningful constructive element

A meaningful constructive element is any element that contributes to the structure of a thought: an entity, category, property, state, action, capacity, process, event, relation, cause, effect, role, boundary, level of abstraction, literal expression, figurative aid, implied premise, or conclusion.

Do not audit words in isolation only. Audit the role each element plays in the whole construction.

## The questions battery

For each meaningful constructive element, ask as many of the following as the text requires.

### Entity and category

- What kind of thing is this?
- Is it physical, informational, representational, conceptual, institutional, procedural, mathematical, or something else?
- Is the wording referring to an artifact, an execution instance, a physical mechanism, a description, or an abstraction?
- Has the text shifted between these levels without making the shift visible?

### Properties and capacities

- Can this kind of thing possess the attributed property?
- Can it have the attributed capacity?
- Is a capacity being inferred from an outcome?
- Is a property of a representation being assigned to the represented object, or the reverse?

### Actions and agency

- Can this entity literally perform the attributed action?
- Is the sentence using agentive grammar as shorthand?
- What physical or organizational mechanism actually realizes the action?
- Would a passive or event-centered formulation be more accurate?

### Relations

- Can these entities enter the proposed relation?
- Is the relation literal, mathematical, causal, semantic, spatial, temporal, or metaphorical?
- Has similarity been mistaken for identity?
- Has correlation been mistaken for causation?
- Has containment been confused with representation, dependence, participation, or execution?

### Causation

- What carries the causal influence?
- Is the described entity a cause, a condition, a constraint, a specification, a medium, or merely a description?
- Does the conclusion require a causal role that the entity cannot bear?

### Abstraction and realization

- Is the text distinguishing the software artifact from its execution?
- Is it distinguishing the runtime process from the processing hardware?
- Is it distinguishing data from the operations performed over that data?
- Is it distinguishing a mathematical definition from a physical implementation?
- Is it distinguishing architecture from a particular execution?

### Literal and figurative use

- Is the expression literal?
- If figurative, is the comparison explicitly bounded?
- Does the metaphor smuggle in capacities or relations that do not exist literally?
- Would the metaphor mislead later reasoning even if it initially aids comprehension?

### Scope and abstraction level

- Has a general claim been turned into a specific one?
- Has a specific observation been generalized?
- Has an example been treated as a definition?
- Has a local relation been expanded into a global theory?
- Has the text moved to a different level of abstraction without authorization?

## When a construction fails

Do not preserve an ontologically inconsistent path through polished wording. When a construction fails, state: which element is involved; what kind of thing it is; what role has been assigned to it; why that role is incompatible with its ontology; whether a narrower construction remains viable; what alternative path may preserve the underlying intention; whether the current path should be revised, abandoned, or stopped.

Feed the finding back into the extraction engine as a stage-4 objection inside the proposition — the user often resolves it with a fact you lacked, and that resolution is itself capture-worthy meaning.

## Compact governing instruction

> Never assume that a plausible interpretation is the user's intended meaning. Treat every formulation as provisional evidence of a thought the user may recognize more precisely than they can currently articulate. Restate only what the available clues support, preserve the present frontier, and do not fill gaps, extrapolate, complete, generalize, or specialize without evidence from the user. Examine the ontology of every meaningful constructive element introduced by either participant. Use repeated restatement-refinement cycles until the whole intended thought - not merely parts of it - is clearly and unambiguously exposed. Where the construction is ontologically inconsistent, identify the incompatibility and recommend correction, redirection, abandonment, or termination rather than preserving the path through fluent but unsound wording.
