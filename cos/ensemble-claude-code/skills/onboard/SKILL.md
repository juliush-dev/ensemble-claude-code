# Onboard — read the project's face before touching it

This is the retrieval protocol's onboarding moment: a project's notebook geography exists so a reader can stop at the right level, and this runbook walks that descent in order.

## The read, in order

0. **Litter first.** A `LITTER-FLAG.md` at the project root means a previous session ended with uncommitted changes; settle it per its own text (review the state, form coherent commits or deliberately discard, delete the flag) before or alongside the work. Route-level litter flags are the pass-discipline runbook's business at pickup.
1. **The constitution** — the project's designation instrument, at the opening of its notebook (`notebook/Constitution.md` under the project folder, per the anatomy in `10-work-object-model.md`; a project showing no `notebook/` is not wrapped — see the honesty notes). Take from it: what this project is (identity), what constrains all work in it (bounds), and where its notebook regions live (the notebook declaration). The bounds bind you from this moment.
2. **The aim surface** — `Aim.md` beside the constitution. Take from it: the founding wish as currently sharpened, the envisioned body-state, and what is ripening. This tells you what the project wants now — which is not always what the dispatch assumed.
3. **The registry** — the roster of the project's pursuits (contracts, under `pursuits/`) and tendings (charters, under `tendings/`), `notebook/Registry.md`. Take from it: what is live, what is agreed, what is suspended or retired. Tendings especially — their failure mode is invisibility, and the registry is where they stay visible.

## Descend no deeper than the task needs

The face is enough to orient. Go deeper only where the task points:

- Working a specific pursuit → its contract kernel, then its spec and plan.
- Picking up a route → stop; that is the `pass-discipline` runbook's binding pickup read, not onboarding.
- A tending's beat → its charter, then the `occurrence` runbook.

## Honesty notes

- If the project is not wrapped — no `notebook/`, `body/`, `inbox/` repositories, or the face (constitution, aim, registry) missing from `notebook/` — say so rather than inventing a shape silently, and make the offer to wrap first, before any work: name what wrapping would take (plain wrapping, or fusion, absorption, renaming, restructuring — the `wrap` runbook), and ask through the Concertmaster. The offer is non-blocking to answer — a question still gets its answer — and blocking to work: on yes, wrap per the `wrap` runbook (for an adopted body, the geography declaration and the survey of its existing governance surfaces), then serve; on no, stand down, serving nothing in that project. The shared external brain is the sole exception, where work may happen unwrapped. Until his word, captures land on a native surface or one clearly marked file, never nowhere — the locality law applied to a project with no maintained surface yet: land it in the tree it arose in, on one clearly marked file, and port it when the wrap gives it a home. Until his word the project's own pre-existing governance surfaces — backlogs, handoffs, docs — stay authoritative, and no ensemble-shaped structure is invented ahead of it.
- If the face contradicts what the dispatch assumed, surface the contradiction before working; the project's own designation outranks the dispatch's phrasing.
- If notebook surfaces are found nested inside the body — a `docs/` folder, wholly or partly, and even where the notebook declaration itself names those locations — the project is not wrapped as the anatomy prescribes: surface the misplacement as a wrap need before working, through the same offer; relocation is gated work, never silent, and a broken geography is never followed as if lawful.
